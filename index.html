How to Improve Your Typing Speed

Improving your typing speed is less about talent and more about consistent, deliberate practice. Most people type between 40 and 60 words per minute — professional typists and programmers often reach 80 to 100 WPM or more. The difference comes down to technique and repetition.

The single most important thing you can do is learn to touch type — typing without looking at the keyboard. This feels slow at first but unlocks dramatic speed gains once the muscle memory sets in. Start with the home row keys (ASDF JKL;) and build outward from there.

Accuracy matters more than speed. Rushing leads to errors which actually slows you down. Focus on hitting every key correctly and your speed will naturally increase over time. A good rule of thumb: if your accuracy drops below 95%, slow down.

Top 5 Tips for Faster Typing

1. Use all ten fingers
Assign each finger to specific keys and never break that rule. It feels unnatural at first but becomes automatic within a few weeks.
2. Keep your eyes on the screen
Resist the urge to look down at your keyboard. Looking down breaks your rhythm and prevents muscle memory from forming.
3. Practice for 15 minutes daily
Short daily sessions beat long occasional ones. Consistency builds muscle memory far faster than marathon practice sessions.
4. Slow down to speed up
When learning new keys or patterns, go slow enough to be accurate every time. Speed follows accuracy automatically.
5. Track your progress
Seeing improvement over time is the best motivation. Use SumnuTyping's history chart to watch your WPM climb week over week.

What is WPM and How is it Calculated?

WPM stands for Words Per Minute — the standard measurement of typing speed. But a "word" in typing tests is not a real dictionary word. Instead, it is defined as five keystrokes, including spaces and punctuation. This standardization makes it fair to compare speeds across different texts.

To calculate your WPM, divide the total number of characters you typed correctly by 5 (to get word equivalents), then divide by the number of minutes elapsed. SumnuTyping calculates this in real time as you type, updating your WPM counter every second.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is SumnuTyping completely free?
Yes, 100% free. All typing tests, games, lessons, and the global leaderboard are completely free with no hidden costs or premium tiers. The site is supported by advertising.
Do I need to create an account?
No account is needed to practice typing or play games. However, creating a free account lets you post scores to the global leaderboard, save your progress history, and earn achievement badges.
How long does it take to improve typing speed?
Most people see noticeable improvement within 2 to 4 weeks of daily practice. Going from 40 WPM to 70 WPM typically takes 1 to 3 months of consistent 15-minute daily sessions. Learning proper touch typing technique first is the fastest path to high speeds.
What is a good typing speed?
The average adult types between 40 and 60 WPM. A typing speed of 65 to 75 WPM is considered professional. Many jobs that require data entry or transcription expect at least 60 WPM. Programmers and writers who type all day often reach 80 to 100 WPM.
What are the typing games on SumnuTyping?
SumnuTyping features three typing games: Word Fall (type falling words before they hit the bottom), Survival (type words against a countdown timer with increasing difficulty), and Type Racer (race against your personal ghost to beat your own best time). All games have Easy, Medium, and Hard difficulty levels.
Can I use SumnuTyping to prepare for a job interview typing test?
Absolutely. SumnuTyping's timed tests closely replicate the format of professional typing assessments. Practice the 60-second test mode regularly and aim for consistent accuracy above 95%. You can also export your progress report to show employers your improvement over time.
How does the global leaderboard work?
After completing a typing test, signed-in users can post their score to the global leaderboard. Only each user's personal best score is displayed. You can filter the leaderboard by today, this week, all time, or by specific test duration.

Why Typing Speed Matters

In a world where knowledge work happens almost entirely on keyboards, typing speed is a genuine professional skill. A person who types 80 WPM completes the same written work in half the time of someone who types 40 WPM. Over a full working day that adds up to hours of extra productivity.

For developers and programmers, fast typing means faster code iteration. For writers and journalists, it means thoughts flow from brain to screen without bottleneck. For students, it means faster note-taking and better exam performance. For customer service and data entry roles, minimum typing speed requirements are often listed in job descriptions.

Beyond professional benefits, fast accurate typing simply feels great. There is a reason the mechanical keyboard community has millions of passionate members — the tactile satisfaction of flying across keys without thinking is genuinely enjoyable. SumnuTyping exists to help you get there, one test at a time.

How to use this screen

Timed, Words, and Code are not the same drill

Timed starts a clock on the first key and stops at 15 seconds, 30 seconds, 60 seconds, or 2 minutes. Words hides that clock and ends when you finish the passage. Code swaps the passage for short programming lines. Live WPM counts correct characters divided by five, divided by minutes. Accuracy is correct keys divided by every key. A miss is marked and the cursor moves on.

If accuracy is under 95 percent, slow down or drop a level. The formula, a worked example, and the speed bands are in the WPM guide, not repeated here. Lessons are the place to learn a row. Games are a different kind of pressure.
